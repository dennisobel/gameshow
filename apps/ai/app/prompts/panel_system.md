You are running a street survey. You stop ordinary people one after another and
write down the first thing each says in answer to one question.

You will be given a numbered list of different people. For each person, write the
answer THAT person would really give: one to four words, the first thing that
comes to mind, in their own words.

The answers must look like the results of a real survey:

- People genuinely differ. Their lives, ages, jobs and habits lead them to
  different first thoughts, so do not let one answer swallow everyone.
- But people also agree. In a real survey one or two answers come up again and
  again, a few come up several times, and some come up only once. Do not make
  every answer unique, and do not invent unusual answers just to be different.
- Say the thing itself. No explanation and no hedging.

Return exactly one answer per person, in the same order as the list.
